About

Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded research project developing theoretical foundations for mechanistic interpretability.

We aim to produce theoretical and foundational results in mechanistic interpretability by leveraging the recent advances in the mathematical abilities of frontier models. To make sure that the theorems provided by the frontier models do not contain logical flaws, we formally verify them in Lean 4.

Preliminary analyses hint at a mathematical abstraction (imagine Feynman diagrams for particle physics and more recently, twistors for cosmological correlators) for multihead attention that can unlock progress in knowledge discovery for foundational mechanistic interpretability. Such an abstraction would help with tracking features across the layers of a transformer and hence provide theoretical foundations to build tools for feature discovery in transformers.

We start with a simple pilot question: how many attention heads are required to represent a Boolean function? The setting is simple enough for frontier models to make progress while requiring a human expert to ask the high level questions.

Approach

01

Frontier models

An autoresearch pipeline, consisting of multiple frontier models collaborating to generate theorems and formally verify them. Our implementation adapts and extends the harness proposed in Numina Lean Agent, tailoring it to the problem at hand.

02

Lean 4

Unverified automation can fail quietly. Frontier models make mistakes, long derivations accumulate subtle errors, and human evaluation alone cannot reliably catch either. Requiring every proof to pass the Lean kernel removes this failure mode for mathematical claims.

03

Empirical grounding

The pilot question grew out of MATS work on understanding alignment pretraining, studying how features are represented after an attention update. This gives some evidence that the expressivity of multihead attention is relevant to empirical mech interp, not just to formal expressivity theory.

Research

Rigorous, machine-checked theory of what transformer components can and cannot represent.

Most of what the field knows about model internals is empirical and informal. We are building a growing body of verified theorems about what transformer components can and cannot represent, together with the multi-agent system that produces them.

01

Mech interp

Mechanistic interpretability is an important component of a broader toolkit for AI safety and oversight. Theoretical mech interp works (Elhage et al., 2021; Elhage et al., 2022) have shaped empirical interpretability by providing useful abstractions, and principled starting points for experimentation.

However, developing this kind of theory has been demanding, requiring sustained expert effort to work through the underlying mathematics.

  • Pilot question

    How many attention heads are required to represent a Boolean function? We first treat multihead attention as a basic building block and then extend this analysis by including feed forward networks.

  • Composition

    Working towards the goal of composing these component level theorems to understand the expressivity of the multilayer transformers.

  • Precedent

    Our aim is more modest than that of Elhage et al. but similar in spirit: a growing library of kernel-checked theorems that applied researchers can use as reference points.

02

Verified theory

The pilot problem is head complexity H*(f), the minimum number of attention heads a single-layer, attention-only transformer needs to compute a Boolean function. Prove lower and upper bounds, compute it for natural families, and ask whether it equals a known invariant.

  • Lower bounds

    If f has a 2-bit checkerboard restriction then H*(f) ≥ 2: one head can never separate one diagonal of a restricted 2-cube from the other. Head complexity also dominates threshold degree.

  • Constructive upper bounds

    Every symmetric threshold is computable with a single head, so H*(AND) = H*(OR) = H*(MAJORITY) = 1. Weighted-sum interpolation bounds every symmetric function by n heads and every Boolean function by 2ⁿ − 1.

  • Exact classifications

    H*(XORₙ) = n: in this model, parity needs exactly one head per input bit. For symmetric functions, one head buys exactly one sign change along the Hamming-weight axis.

  • Strict separation

    An explicit ten-bit function has deg±(f₁₀) = 2 < 3 ≤ H*(f₁₀). Threshold degree is a genuine lower bound, not an exact characterization of head complexity.

03

Autoresearch systems

An open source multi agent orchestration harness: a system for coordinating frontier models for conjecture generation, proof search and Lean verification.

No single frontier model closes the loop alone on this problem, but the right combinations do. We are mapping which orchestration choices unlock mathematical progress, and publishing the harness and ablations, negative results included.

  • Conjecture generation

    A lead agent re-plans the open frontier from what is now proved, then picks one concrete, provable target: a new lemma, a tighter general bound, a separation, or a new invariant.

  • Mathematical critique

    Candidate invariants must reproduce every already-proven value, then survive an adversarial counterexample hunt. A false conjecture sunk into Lean wastes the whole formalization.

  • Proof search

    Generate, verify, refine, repeat. One model drafts proofs, one surveys the literature, and a panel verifies. A result is established only when the verifier passes it.

  • Lean verification

    The throughline: agents shorten the path to a candidate proof, and the Lean build is the only oracle. Its kernel rejects any hallucinated step.

Project description

The theorem stack

deg±(f) ≤ H*(f) = L_frac(f) ≤ C₊(f) ≤ M₊(f) − 1

Whenever the left and right sides meet, the value is exact.

Dependency structure

A ledger of the main theorems, their status, and how they fit together. Most lemmas are machinery for one of these inequalities, or exactness results where a lower bound meets an upper bound.

Lean 4 formalization

The foundational stack is machine-checked for general n, with no sorry or admit, depending only on the three standard Lean axioms [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound].

Epistemic tiers. Lemmas 1–12 are Lean-formalized. The spine ledger entries and certificates are exact-arithmetic-verified where a verify script is cited. The autoresearch entries beyond them are informally verified by a generate-and-verify pipeline: real proofs, but with a weaker check than the Lean tier, and their ledgers record per-entry status.

Open outputs

Every proof is verified by Lean; everything is open source.

  • Repository Xorformer How many attention heads does it take to compute a Boolean function? Theorem ledger, writeups, Lean 4 formalization, and a Python package that estimates H*(f) empirically.
  • Writeup How many attention heads do you need to do XOR? You need at least two attention heads to do XOR, and we will find that it is a surprisingly crisp result which uses only a few lines of algebra.
