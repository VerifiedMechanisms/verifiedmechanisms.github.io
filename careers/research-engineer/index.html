Harness design

Research Engineer

Build the harness that makes several frontier models produce mathematics a proof kernel will accept, and make every run legible enough to learn from. This is a paid role on a three-month project.

The project

Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded research project developing theoretical foundations for mechanistic interpretability. An autoresearch pipeline of multiple frontier models collaborates to generate theorems and formally verify them in Lean 4, adapting and extending the harness proposed in Numina Lean Agent.

Over the course of the project we hope to identify the “secret sauce” of multiagent orchestration: the harness that can actually make progress in foundational mechanistic interpretability. This also derisks the project. Even if the mathematics advances more slowly than hoped, the funded work still leaves behind an open, reusable multi-agent system for formalized interpretability theory.

Single models working alone did not converge on this problem. Several models orchestrated to collaborate, one drafting proofs, one surveying the literature, and a panel verifying, produced correct, machine-checked Lean code.

That observation is what the orchestration study tests: no single frontier model closes the loop alone here, but the right combinations do.

What you will do

  • Build the framework that coordinates agents to do the math.
  • Integrate Lean compilation into the agent loop.
  • Build benchmarks and compare models, prompts, agent roles, and coordination strategies based on verified mathematical progress.
  • Build Inspect logging over the autoresearch runs, so that every conjecture, critique, proof attempt and verification verdict in a multi-hour run is easy to browse, diff and compare across ablations.

You will have substantial autonomy over your daily work. The research lead sets the overall research direction, reviews your work, and helps resolve mathematical or engineering blockers.

Requirements

For all applicants

  • Able to commit around 15 hours per week, attend weekly meetings, and make independent progress between meetings.
  • Proficient with Python and Git, including reading an unfamiliar codebase, and making sense of it.
  • Comfortable using LLM coding agents critically. Applicants must be able to inspect and explain agent-generated work rather than treating it as authoritative.

For this role

  • Experience building and running scalable agentic workflows on HPC-like environments.
  • Experience designing controlled evaluations on multi-agent workflows.
  • Prior experience with Lean or another proof assistant is helpful but not required.

Screening task

The screening task asks you to choose a self-contained mathematical problem that two frontier models do not reliably solve independently, but can solve through collaboration, and to submit a public repository containing a reproducible collaboration framework and complete transcripts of each model alone and of the two collaborating. The README should explain the problem and the prompts, the failure modes of the independent runs, how information was exchanged during collaboration, and what enabled the collaborative system to succeed.
