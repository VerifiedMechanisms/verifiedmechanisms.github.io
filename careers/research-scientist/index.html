The project

Verified Mechanisms is an independent, grant-funded research project developing theoretical foundations for mechanistic interpretability. LLM agents collaborate to discover and formally verify theorems about the internal computations of transformers, beginning with a simple pilot question: how many attention heads are needed to represent a Boolean function?

Recent advances in language models for mathematical reasoning and formal theorem proving may make it possible to produce rigorous results at a lower cost. This project aims to produce foundational results for empirical interpretability by leveraging the growing mathematical abilities of frontier models, while identifying multi agent harnesses that make such theoretical research faster and more reliable.

What you will do

  • Read and interpret the outputs from autoresearch runs, identifying promising conjectures, hidden assumptions and useful directions.
  • Turn promising outputs into precise research questions and experiments.
  • Work with the research engineer to improve the autoresearch workflow.
  • Contribute towards public writeups.

You will have substantial autonomy over your daily work. The research lead sets the overall research direction, reviews your work, and helps resolve mathematical or engineering blockers.

The autoresearch ledger already runs to nearly two hundred results across lower bounds, upper bounds, exact classifications and separations, of which the foundational stack is machine-checked in Lean 4. The collection contains many lemmas of varying scope and significance, so a large part of this role is research judgment: identifying the small subset that matters most.

Requirements

For all applicants

  • Able to commit at least 10 hours per week, attend weekly meetings, and make independent progress between meetings.
  • Proficient with Python and Git, including reading an unfamiliar codebase, and making sense of it.
  • Comfortable using LLM coding agents critically. Applicants must be able to inspect and explain agent-generated work rather than treating it as authoritative.

For this role

  • Strong mathematical background, including experience writing proofs in an (applied) mathematical setting.
  • Familiarity with mechanistic interpretability, or other relevant areas of mathematical machine learning.
  • Ability to critically evaluate LLM-generated research outputs, identify promising conjectures and arguments, and pick out the substantive insights.
  • Prior experience with Lean or another proof assistant is helpful but not required.

Selection process

Résumé review → screening task → interview.

The screening task is issued after résumé review. It asks you to review a collection of theorems generated during an autoresearch run, identify the two or three results you consider most important, restate each claim in your own words, explain the main mathematical idea behind it, and discuss what further questions or research directions it suggests. The writeup should demonstrate independent understanding of the mathematics rather than simply paraphrasing the generated text.
